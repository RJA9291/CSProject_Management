SCENE 3 – WHAT IS E-HAZOP?  [00:48.8–01:22.9]
Visuals: Full-term reveal, HAZOP-to-E-HAZOP bridge (IEC 61882), animated single-line diagram.

[00:49.4] E-HAZOP stands for Electrical Hazard and Operability Study.
[00:53.2] It is a systematic and structured technique used to identify potential hazards, operational problems and deviations within electrical systems.
[01:01.7] It applies the principles of the traditional HAZOP methodology to electrical designs.
[01:06.8] Rather than simply inspecting individual equipment, E-HAZOP examines how the overall electrical system is designed and how different components interact.
[01:15.7] The objective is to identify possible problems early, assess their consequences and recommend appropriate safeguards.

SCENE 4 – HOW DOES E-HAZOP WORK?  [01:22.9–02:04.1]
Visuals: Five-step workflow, team review of the SLD, nodes, guideword × parameter matrix, hazard register.

[01:23.6] So, how does E-HAZOP work?
[01:25.6] First, the engineering team reviews important design documents, such as electrical single-line diagrams.
[01:32.0] Next, the electrical system is divided into smaller sections, known as nodes.
[01:36.8] The team then applies guidewords to electrical parameters such as voltage, current, power, frequency and earthing.
[01:43.2] For example, guidewords such as 'over', 'under', 'spike', 'dip' and 'fault' help the team consider possible deviations from normal operating conditions.
[01:51.7] Each deviation is analysed by identifying its possible causes, consequences and existing protective safeguards.
[01:58.8] Finally, recommendations and corrective actions are documented in a hazard register.

SCENE 5 – PRACTICAL EXAMPLE: TRANSFORMER OVERVOLTAGE (ILLUSTRATIVE)  [02:04.1–02:37.0]
Visuals: Transformer, rising-voltage trend, relay alarm, insulation stress, cause–consequence–safeguard table.

[02:04.5] Consider a practical example involving a power transformer.
[02:08.6] What if the transformer experiences an overvoltage condition?
[02:12.4] This deviation could be caused by incorrect protection settings or disturbances within the electrical network.
[02:18.6] If not properly controlled, it may place stress on the insulation and potentially damage the equipment.
[02:24.4] During an E-HAZOP study, the team examines the possible causes, consequences and effectiveness of existing protection systems.
[02:31.9] The team can then recommend additional safeguards or corrective actions where necessary.

SCENE 6 – WHY IS E-HAZOP IMPORTANT?  [02:37.0–03:04.5]

[02:37.4] E-HAZOP plays an important role in improving electrical safety and system reliability.
[02:42.9] It helps protect personnel, reduce the likelihood of equipment damage, identify design weaknesses and support business continuity.
[02:50.5] It is particularly valuable during the design stage, as well as when electrical systems undergo modifications or major upgrades.
[02:58.3] A multidisciplinary team is essential, bringing together engineering, operations and maintenance knowledge.
